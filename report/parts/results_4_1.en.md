All four models ran to 12,000 steps and {{ n(r.training.swa.tokens_seen) }} tokens with no divergence, no loss spikes and no restarts. Gradient clipping rarely bound: after warm-up, the outer-loop gradient norm exceeded the threshold of 1.0 on {{ pct(r.training.swa.clipped_fraction, 1) }}% of logged steps for sliding-window attention and {{ pct(r.training.ttt.clipped_fraction, 1) }}% for TTT-E2E.

Training losses are not comparable across the four. Full attention can read the whole window at every position during training and so has a lower training loss by construction, while sliding-window attention sees only the last 512 positions; and TTT-E2E's training loss is measured *after* its fast weights have been updated the way they will be at test time, which is a different quantity again. The left panel of Figure 2 serves only to confirm that all four runs converged cleanly. The comparable quantities are in the middle and right panels: one evaluation protocol, one set of held-out windows.

TTT-E2E's inner loop stays active throughout training. The fast-weight norm grows from {{ f1(r.training.ttt.initial_fast_weight_norm) }} at initialisation to {{ f1(r.training.ttt.final_fast_weight_norm) }}, each inner update has a norm of about {{ f2(r.training.ttt.final_inner_update_norm) }}, and the inner gradient norm sits near {{ f2(r.training.ttt.final_inner_grad_norm) }} — slightly above the clipping threshold \(\tau=1\), meaning most update steps are clipped to a fixed step size. What the outer loop learns is therefore which direction to move in at a fixed step size, rather than how far to move. Section 4.8's inner-learning-rate ablation shows the corresponding effect.
