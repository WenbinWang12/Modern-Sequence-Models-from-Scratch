The evaluation window is stretched from 4,096 to 32,768 tokens — eight times the training length — with no fine-tuning and no change to the models. Full attention cannot take part in this comparison: its position encoding was only ever trained within 4,096, so extrapolating to 32K leaves the training distribution. We measured it separately at 8,192 and 16,384; see `full_extrapolation` in `results.json`.

One distinction matters here. Neither sliding-window attention nor TTT-E2E has a length-extrapolation problem: every layer sees only the last 512 positions, so the relative distances their position encoding meets at 32K are exactly those seen in training. This figure therefore does not measure whether longer inputs can be processed, but whether the fast weights keep accumulating useful information as more is read. The widening advantage says the inner loop still works, without diverging, at eight times the training length.{% endif %}
