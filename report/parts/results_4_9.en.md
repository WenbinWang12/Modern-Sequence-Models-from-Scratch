The seed experiment changes both initialisation and data order while keeping batch alignment between models within a seed. Paired differences in Table 11 are more informative than separate means because they remove variation shared by a data order. The seed count remains limited, so the reported standard deviations describe stability at this scale rather than a universal uncertainty estimate.

The scaling experiment changes depth and width under a fixed training-token budget and context setting. Table 12 asks whether the gaps between TTT, sliding-window and full attention change with capacity. It does not by itself support a reliable scaling law. Larger models and longer training require new budgets, data audits and independent seeds.
